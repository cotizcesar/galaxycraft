# Terran campaign map (Episode I and Episode V)

What each Terran mission uses and how it was checked, so the next pass can
pick up from here.  The table is generated by `scripts/campaign_census.py`
from the retail `scenario.chk` files (the install's cache); the findings
come from loading every mission in `sc_view` (`--shot-map N --game-script`)
and from the OpenBW source.

Client map indices: Episode I is 0 (Boot Camp) to 12 (The Hammer Falls),
Episode V is 35 (First Strike) to 43 (To Chain the Beast).

## How the missions were checked

1. **Triggers.**  Every condition and action kind used by the 22 maps
   (and their `MBRF` briefing triggers) is handled by `trigger.rs`,
   leaderboards included.
2. **AI scripts.**  Every script the triggers start decodes, and every
   opcode it uses has a handler in `aiscript.rs` (checked with
   `examples/ai_opcodes.rs`).
3. **Runs.**  All 22 missions were loaded and run for 2 000, 4 000 and
   12 000 ticks with the player idle.  None crashes, none ends by itself,
   and the computer players build and attack (the player loses units in
   Desperate Alliance, Assault on Korhal and Birds of War).  Screenshots
   checked at the start and after 1 000 ticks.
4. **Objectives.**  `sc_solve` (`src/bin/sc_solve.rs`) plays every mission
   to its end through the real trigger VM and campaign AIs.  It reads
   the human's goal conditions from the triggers: bring units to a
   location, command / kill counts, deaths, resources.  It satisfies them
   in stage order (the mission's own switches decide which goal comes
   next), but it does not steer units: it moves or spawns the units
   itself.  The player has invincibility (`power overwhelming`), and
   enemies are killed by script once they have been seen.  So it shows
   that every chain of triggers can reach victory; it does not show that
   a human can win the mission.

   ```text
   sc_solve --install "$SC_INSTALL" --episode terran|expterran [--mission KEY]
   ```

   Result (60 000-tick cap, 2026-11-13): **all 22 missions end in
   VICTORY** fired by their own victory trigger.  Episode I at ticks
   722 (tutorial), 30, 3218, 2502, 705, 2010, 1969, 2010, 741, 2772,
   2689, 3271, 2010; Episode V at 2355, 6540, 2691, 2284, 5041 (5a),
   5371 (5b), 4056, 859, 3084.  The Dylarian Shipyards needs its full
   eight-switch battlecruiser chain, civilian deaths included.

## The computer's pace with the player idle

`SOLVE_IDLE=1 sc_solve --episode E` runs each mission with no cheat and no
solver and reports when an enemy first hurts (ATTACK) or kills (LOSS) one
of the player's units (2026-11-13, 30 000-tick cap, ~21 min at Fastest):

| Episode I | first hit | Episode V | first hit |
|---|---|---|---|
| tutorial, 1, 2, 4 | none (no enemy script) | 1 | attack 103 s |
| 3 Desperate Alliance | attack 56 s | 2 | attack 27 s |
| 5 | loss 529 s | 3 | loss at once (scripted) |
| 6 Norad II | none (scripts wait for the player) | 4 | attack 120 s |
| 7 | attack 161 s | 5a / 5b | attack 49 s / 28 s |
| 8 The Trump Card | attack 15 s | 6 | loss at once (scripted) |
| 9 | attack 146 s | 7 | none (no town script) |
| 10 | attack 147 s | 8 | loss at once (scripted) |
| 11 | attack 285 s | | |
| 12 The Hammer Falls | attack 372 s | | |

Missions with no hit start no AI script for an enemy (Backwater Station's
15 triggers have no Run AI Script action), so its units only hold their
ground, as in retail, where a campaign computer runs only the scripts its
triggers start (checked in retail: five idle minutes, no attack).  Norad
II starts its two Zerg scripts only from switch 10, which the player's
own progress sets (bringing the Command Center, the first deaths);
Patriot's Blood runs only `Enmy` and `Rscu`.

Desperate Alliance compared with retail (live client, 2026-11-13, player
idle, frames): retail's two Zerglings of `attack_add 2` were at the
player's ramp at ~1170 and hit at ~1280.  Ours hit at 1344: the party
walks out after `attack_prepare` (`src/aiscript/attack_prep.rs`) and
leaves at the `attack_do` (1250).  The other missions are not compared
with retail yet.

Two script bugs this table found (0.1.41): `upgrade` / `tech` held the
script thread until the research could start, so a town with one busy
Engineering Bay never reached its attacks (The Hammer Falls never
attacked; Silent Scream's first hit came at 924 s, now 147 s); they are
queued town requests now (`src/aiscript/research.rs`).  With no enemy
town hall, the attack target was the nearest unit of any other owner
(allies and neutrals too); it is the nearest hostile one now.  `sc_solve`
also passes the human's town hall as the target, as `sc_view` does (it
passed none).

0.1.42: `build` is a lasting town request too (`src/aiscript/requests.rs`):
a `build` the town could not afford at that tick was forgotten, so a later
`wait_build` for it waited forever and lost buildings were never rebuilt.
The idle table does not change (the towns start rich); it matters once the
player hurts the town.

0.1.43: a stall check (an AI script on the same instruction for 5000
ticks, not in a `wait`, all six episodes) found `defensebuild_*` holding
the script until the defender could be trained: Biting the Bullet's two
towns (first hit 395 s, now 161 s), the Protoss tutorial and Brood War
Protoss 3.  It is a town request now; no script stalls left with the
player idle.

0.1.44, The Trump Card against retail (live client, 2026-11-13, player
idle, screenshots every 2 s): the player's Control Tower is hit from the
east at ~11 s and burns at 13 s; supply drops 22 -> 21 at ~21 s and to 19
at ~45 s.  Ours hit first at 38 s.  The cause was a unit mix-up: trigger
locations are map pixels, but `location_center` divided them by 256, so
every script started "at location" (`EnBk`, `move_dt`, `value_area`,
`player_enemy` / `player_ally`, nukes, Disruption Web) aimed near the
map's top-left corner; `region_at_tile` also got pixels where it wants
tiles.  `enter_bunker` used the first Bunker on the map and an 8-pixel
search square; it now fills the Bunker nearest its location with the
infantry within 8 tiles.  `player_enemy` / `player_ally` pushed unit
indices as player numbers.  Ours now hits first at 15 s (Bunkers filled
when the 22-second trigger runs `EnBk`).  What hits the retail Control
Tower at 11 s is not identified; ours is not hit in the first 50 s.

| problem | missions | fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rescue measured centre to centre (64 px): units a mission expects to change hands at once stayed rescuable, and a defeat trigger fired | Brood War Zerg 8 (defeat on tick 1), Protoss 5 (Artanis, tick 49) | gap between the `units.dat` boxes, per axis, ≤ 36 px (`sim/alliances.rs`; INFERENCE, see `pending.md`) |
| Map resources are kept under player 1, so "Player 1 commands at most 0 buildings" never held while a Vespene Geyser stood; Set Resources (action 52) on player 12 found nothing | Protoss tutorial, Protoss 1, Protoss 3 | triggers see resources as player 12's (`sim::trigger_owner`) |
| After a Wait the trigger pass started over at trigger 0, so a preserved trigger with a Wait ran again before any later trigger, forever | Brood War Zerg 1 | the pass goes on after the trigger that waited, also when the Wait ends between trigger cycles (`trigger.rs`; INFERENCE) |

The rest were solver gaps (goals it did not yet read): "at most / exactly
N in a location", enemy units to clear from a location, unit classes in
"commands at most 0", one hero needed at several beacons in turn, a goal
on another player's hero.

## Open

- Alliances are one team per player, so they are transitive; retail keeps
  pairs.  No Terran mission puts the human in an allied force with a
  computer, so the campaign is not affected.
- The trigger chains of all 68 missions were driven to victory by
  `sc_solve`, but not by a human with normal units: how hard each mission is and whether
  the computer's attacks are timed like retail is not checked.
- The `defenseuse` response timing (every 24 ticks, idle units only) is an
  inference.
